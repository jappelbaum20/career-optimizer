# Career workflows

Read this reference for fit, CVs, LinkedIn, letters, experience intake,
applications, networking, visibility, job discovery, or funnel review.
Use `writing-and-qa.md` for every external-facing draft and
`interviews-and-negotiation.md` for interviews or compensation.

## Start from the live career record

- Read the Notion Career HQ and Profile page for confirmed facts,
  preferences, languages, discretion, disclosure boundaries and current goals.
- Query relevant Experience records for achievements and personal contribution.
- For a specific role, read its Opportunities record and linked Activities.
- Documents is optional: use it for references to templates and saved artifacts.
- Use `notion-schema.md` for properties, relations and allowed statuses;
  this reference does not define a competing schema.
- A past CV, LinkedIn snapshot or conversation can supply evidence, but does
  not silently override a more recent confirmed record. Resolve conflicts.
- Missing onboarding detail should prompt focused questions, not prevent a
  useful draft grounded in the available evidence.
- Distinguish drafts, confirmed facts, reported events and successful saves.
  Never claim a save, submission or sent message without evidence.

## Job fit

1. Read the supplied posting or fetch the exact URL. Record source and date.
   If inaccessible, use pasted content and identify what remains unavailable.
2. Extract responsibilities, explicit requirements, preferences, location,
   work arrangement, deadlines and compensation if stated. Preserve a concise
   posting summary so the role remains understandable if the URL disappears.
3. Map important requirements to Experience evidence: direct match,
   transferable evidence, known gap, or unknown. Link the supporting records.
4. Separate an employer's explicit eligibility condition from a preference or
   an inferred convention. Verify current legal/work-rights claims when needed.
5. Give a qualitative assessment using the schema's Strong / Possible / Stretch / Excluded / Unknown labels, explain
   the strongest evidence and material gaps, and suggest a practical next step.
6. Recommend which achievements to lead with and a possible letter angle.
   Do not equate keyword coverage with hiring probability or an ATS score.

Judge the specific role and the user's goals. Do not reject whole sectors,
levels, career changes or stretch applications by default. If evidence is
incomplete, state uncertainty instead of treating absence as lack of skill.

## CV and resume work

1. Establish general refresh versus tailoring to a specific posting, required
   language, format, requested attachments and submission constraints.
2. Use the user's chosen baseline or closest relevant master from Documents
   when available. Preserve original assets; make a distinguishable revision.
3. Select evidence by relevance to the role, not by prestige or monetary size
   alone. Make the current role understandable even if it lacks headline metrics.
4. Draft a brief summary only when it adds specific positioning. Preserve actual
   employment titles; a target-role label must not imply the user already held it.
5. Build concise bullets from contribution, method, scope and outcome.
   Use numbers only when supported and allowed for the intended audience.
6. Include evidenced skills and accurately labelled credentials. Incorporate
   relevant posting terminology naturally without claiming unheld skills.
7. Choose length and layout for the user, document type and submission context.
   A simple linear reading order is a useful default, not a universal page rule.
8. Run writing and factual QA. For files, inspect the actual export for readable
   text, page breaks, clipping, links and ordering before presenting it.

Deliver the revised text or requested native file, a short explanation of major
changes and any unresolved factual issue. A requirements-to-evidence map can
show coverage; never label a self-made keyword count as an ATS assessment.

## LinkedIn

- Identify the requested section and use its current text or supplied snapshot.
  Check a snapshot's date before treating it as current.
- Headline: communicate professional focus and evidenced strengths in clear
  language; current title, domain words and a differentiator are options.
- About: put the main positioning early, use selected evidence and a suitable
  closing. Narrative or bullets may fit the user's style.
- Experience: show actual scope and contribution; make current work legible
  using relevant Experience records and approved disclosure wording.
- Skills: prioritize demonstrable capabilities aligned with the user's goals.
- If platform limits matter, verify current limits rather than relying on old
  character/skill counts. Do not promise search-ranking effects.
- Offer a before/after view when useful. Drafting does not authorize changing
  the live profile or switching its public job-search settings.

## Cover letters

1. Read the role, company, user motivation and strongest relevant evidence.
   Use a known addressee or a neutral team salutation; do not invent a name.
2. Pick one credible connection between the user's experience and the mandate.
3. Open with the role and that connection. Develop two or three useful evidence
   points rather than retelling the CV or forcing a fixed paragraph template.
4. Show company-specific motivation from the user's account or verified public
   information. Do not invent enthusiasm, values alignment or personal history.
5. Close with the contribution the evidence supports and a suitable next step.
6. Adapt language and format to explicit employer requirements and the user's
   preference. Named projects are optional; approved anonymized evidence works.

Produce a complete draft, with unresolved facts outside the copy-ready artifact.
Never leave template tokens in a draft described as ready to send.

## Experience and project intake

Capture useful detail through a conversation, not a long mandatory form.
Ask only the next few questions needed to understand:

- Role and dates: confirm which employment or other experience this belongs to.
  Do not assign every new project to the current role automatically.
- Context and goal: what needed to change or be delivered?
- Personal contribution: what did the user own, decide, support or build?
- Method and difficulty: tools, judgment, collaboration and constraints.
- Outcome and scope: what changed, what decision was enabled, and what scale
  is evidenced? Distinguish team outcomes from individual attribution.
- Disclosure: which names, figures and details may be stored and used publicly?

When logging is requested, create or update the matching Experience record using
the existing schema and record sources, unknowns and allowed disclosure wording.
Check for an existing record first. Do not silently promote speculative details
to facts. Save only information within the user's authorization and privacy choices.
If a metric would materially improve future writing, record a focused evidence
gap or linked follow-up Activity; qualitative outcomes are valid evidence too.
Suggest relevant CV bullets or an interview story when useful, without updating
every derivative document automatically.

## Applications and status updates

1. Locate or deduplicate the Opportunity using company, role, URL or requisition
   identity. Ambiguous matches require clarification before changing a record.
2. Preserve the posting summary, source and stated deadline with timezone if
   available. Label unstated details unknown rather than estimating them.
3. Assess fit and choose an appropriate degree of customization. A junior role
   or stretch application can deserve careful tailoring; follow the user's ask.
4. Produce requested materials and required attachments. Do not add unnecessary
   documents or fabricate missing certificates, referees or references.
5. Save artifact references through Documents if used, or in the matching
   record using the configured storage workflow. Preserve source versions.
6. Keep the Opportunity in a preparation status until the user reports submission
   or another authoritative source confirms it. Preparing files is not applying.
7. Log authorized updates as linked Activities and update the Opportunity's
   confirmed status and next step using `notion-schema.md`.

For an interview invitation, offer stage-specific prep. For a rejection, capture
the stated reason separately from guesses and draft a response only if useful.
For an offer, continue with the negotiation reference. Do not auto-apply, send,
publish, accept or decline. Scheduling follows the parent skill's explicit
authorization and scheduling workflow; a deadline alone is not scheduling consent.

## Networking and outreach

- Check the Profile's tone and discretion preferences and relevant Activities
  for a warm connection or previous interaction. Do not invent relationships.
- Recruiter reply: connect one supported experience point to the actual role;
  ask material questions about scope, location, process or compensation.
- Cold outreach: use a real shared interest or specific public context, brief
  credibility and one clear request. Avoid fabricated admiration or familiarity.
- Referral ask: supply the exact role and a short forwardable introduction;
  make it easy to decline. Do not imply a referral has been agreed.
- Informational chat: explain the reason for connecting without demanding that
  the user present themselves as a peer if that is not their situation.
- Thank-you note: use a real conversation detail and relevant fit point.
- Follow-up: use the employer's promised timing and context. Suggested timing
  and number of nudges are judgment calls, not universal rules.
- Rejection reply: close respectfully; ask for feedback when appropriate.

Keep drafts brief enough for their channel; verify a hard platform limit when
needed. Use the user's desired register and current conversation tone.
Record an interaction only when logging is authorized. A draft is not a sent
interaction, and composing a message does not authorize sending it.

## Professional content and visibility

Draw topics from evidenced expertise and the audience the user wants to reach.
Possible pillars include practical craft, an explainer, public market developments,
or an approved milestone. These are options, not a required niche or cadence.

Check public-use clearance before using employer/client work. Project completion
and a public announcement do not by themselves authorize attribution of the
user's role or disclosure of internal figures. Anonymization may still reveal
sensitive facts. If safe evidence is unavailable, use a public-source topic.

Draft one useful idea in the user's voice, clearly distinguishing facts, opinion
and predictions. Verify current claims and credit collaborators appropriately.
Use job-search signals only if consistent with the user's chosen visibility.
Tags and hashtags are optional. Drafting never authorizes publication.

## Discovery and funnel review

For job scans, use current Profile targets and user-defined hard filters.
Search current employer career pages or connected sources; verify role identity,
availability, dates and material constraints. Dedupe against Opportunities.
Surface a short list with source links, fit rationale, known gaps and unknowns.
Do not hide all stretch roles unless that is the user's preference.

For search reviews, read Opportunities and Activities over a stated period:

- Separate discovered roles, prepared applications and submitted applications.
- Count confirmed progress through applicable stages and report denominators.
- Treat recent pending applications as pending, not failures. Account for time
  to response and distinguish known rejection from silence.
- Compare sources, role categories or tailoring only where records support it.
  State small samples, missing data and selection bias; do not infer causation.
- Offer a few actions grounded in the evidence, such as improving a recurring
  weak requirement, testing positioning or revisiting the user's chosen targets.

Review stale credentials, disclosure permissions and evidence gaps when relevant.
Do not create recurring reviews or reminders without the scheduling workflow.
