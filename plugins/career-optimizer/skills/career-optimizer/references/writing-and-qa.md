# Writing and quality checks

Use for CVs, LinkedIn, letters, outreach, professional content and interview drafts.
Read the live Profile and relevant Experience records; refer to `notion-schema.md`
for record structure. Source templates contain examples, not user facts.

## Evidence and voice

- Prefer clear contribution, context and result. An outcome + scale + method
  structure can help, but a verified qualitative contribution is equally valid.
- Keep ownership accurate. "Supported", "contributed" and "assisted" may be
  correct; do not replace them with "led", "secured" or "architected" merely
  to sound stronger. Explain what the support achieved when known.
- Do not invent percentages, budgets, users, team sizes, causality, credentials,
  motivation, work rights or proficiency. Preserve ranges and approximate dates.
- Distinguish delivered work from prototypes, plans and ongoing efforts.
  Distinguish forecast savings from realized savings and team outcomes from
  the user's attributable contribution.
- Use recorded language, spelling and tone preferences. Prefer specific nouns
  and plain verbs to promotional claims or generic enthusiasm.
- Preserve exact employer/title/date/degree facts. Translate a title carefully
  when needed without implying a different rank or credential equivalence.

## Disclosure

Read audience-specific confidentiality and disclosure notes in the Profile and
Experience records. Public visibility of an organization does not clear internal
metrics, methods, client relationships or the user's participation for disclosure.
Use approved wording; if unclear, omit sensitive detail or ask a focused question.
Interview conversations also require appropriate confidentiality.
Do not turn an unverified anonymization such as "leading company" into a claim.
Honor the user's storage preferences for private compensation and personal data.

## Reader and application system

Put the role-relevant evidence early and make chronology and scope easy to scan.
An initial skim is a useful review exercise, not a claim that every recruiter
spends a fixed number of seconds screening every document.

Use recognizable headings and a readable text order. A simple single-column CV
is a practical default for automated uploads. Follow the employer's actual file
instructions; do not guarantee universal parser compatibility.
Use relevant JD words only where they accurately describe the user's evidence.
Keyword frequency does not establish employer weighting, screening rules or
hiring probability. Never fabricate an ATS score or promise to beat a system.
If a real authorized parser/report is available, identify what it actually tested
and its limitations rather than generalizing to all application systems.

## Language and market adaptation

Follow explicit posting instructions first, then confirmed user preferences.
Do not assume a language, nationality, location, photo preference or work status
from a filename, example or prior user's profile. Photos, birth dates, signatures
and certificates are context-dependent; include personal details only when
appropriate and authorized. Do not inflate language proficiency from one project.
Check current legal or market claims with authoritative sources when relevant.
Keep technical terms natural in the target language and review false friends.

## Pre-delivery QA

1. Facts: reconcile chronology, titles, qualifications and metrics against live
   sources; remove unsupported inference and stale certification status.
2. Attribution: verify verbs, ownership, outcome status and disclosure clearance.
3. Relevance: can a reader understand the fit, contribution and important gaps?
   Does the letter add useful evidence beyond repeating the CV?
4. Language: check grammar, spelling, register, technical terms and consistency.
5. Artifact: inspect the actual requested file/export, including text extraction,
   page breaks, clipping, links and reading order where applicable. Report any
   unavailable verification honestly; reviewing text alone is not visual QA.
6. Readiness: remove placeholders from finished drafts; keep material questions
   outside reusable copy. Do not describe unresolved factual copy as send-ready.
7. Save: verify successful tool results before reporting a Notion or document
   update. Distinguish a produced draft from a saved artifact or external action.

Make corrections within the authorized editing scope. Report material remaining
limitations concisely. A writing improvement is not a guarantee of an interview.
